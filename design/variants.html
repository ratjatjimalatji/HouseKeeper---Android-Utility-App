HouseKeeper: three design directions

Each direction shows the same four screens so you can compare them directly: Home dashboard, Scanner, Report, and Household. The data is sample data for a fictional household. Use the tabs to switch.

A · Evolved Material

The safest step: grows the app you already have into the new spec.

Idea

  • Keeps today's slate-blue Material 3 look, tonal cards and colour-per-utility, so existing screens need restyling, not rebuilding.
  • Home leads with the most urgent alert, then three one-tap scan tiles, then the quick report.
  • Standard M3 navigation bar, segmented buttons and chips. Closest to stock Compose components, so the fastest to build.

B · Meter Display

Dark, numbers-first. Feels like the meter itself. Built around "how long until it runs out?"

Idea

  • Home opens on a single big countdown (days of electricity left) with a segmented "fuel gauge". Run-out is the headline, not a card.
  • A raised Scan button sits in the middle of the nav bar, so logging is one tap from every tab.
  • The scanner shows the reading digit by digit; any digit the OCR isn't sure about is highlighted and can be tapped to fix, instead of retyping the whole number.
  • Monospaced numerals throughout. Dense, good for people who like data; less friendly for older relatives.

C · Household Notebook

Warm and people-first. Reads like a shared to-do list for the house, not a dashboard.

Idea

  • Home is a "Coming up" timeline: dated things the house needs to do (buy electricity Thursday, order gas next week), each with its action inline.
  • People are visible everywhere: avatars, "Naledi logged this", activity in plain sentences. Best fit for the shared-family goal and remote relatives.
  • Reports open with a one-sentence summary in words, then the graph, then AI suggestions you can rate.
  • Serif headings and a warm palette set it apart from typical utility apps. Moves furthest from today's look.
